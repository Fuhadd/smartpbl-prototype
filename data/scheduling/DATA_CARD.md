# Data card: synthetic scheduling scenario (`scenario.json`)

## Summary

One synthetic day of domiciliary care in inner Birmingham, generated by `scripts/generate_schedule.py` (seed 42). It is the input to every Tier 2 experiment: travel matrix, solver, continuity sweep and greedy baseline.

| Carers | 17 (10 early shift 07:00–14:30, 7 late shift 15:00–22:00) |
| Clients | 40, in 3 geographic patches |
| Visits | 106 (morning 40, lunch 23, tea 12, bed 31) |
| Packages | 4 calls/day: 12 clients · 3 calls: 11 · 2 calls: 8 · 1 call: 9 |

**Load before travel.**
- **Early shift:** 2,505 service minutes against 4,500 carer-minutes (56%).
- **Late shift:** 1,500 against 2,940 (51%).
- **Morning peak:** 40 visits must all start by 10:00. Service time alone is 76% of early-shift capacity between 07:00 and 11:00, and about 90% once roughly 10 minutes' travel per visit is added.

The tight morning peak is deliberate: it is where real rotas break, and it gives the solver's dropped-visit handling something to do.

## How it was generated

1. **Geography.** Three patch centres are drawn around the middle of a bounding box (lat 52.42–52.54, lon −1.98 to −1.78). Clients are scattered around a patch with a Gaussian of about 2 km. Carer homes are scattered about twice as widely. There is one synthetic office in the city-centre area. All coordinates are **rounded to 3 decimal places (~100 m)**, so no point identifies a real dwelling.
2. **Carers.** Each carer gets personal care, plus other skills at fixed rates (medication 70%, manual handling 45%, dementia 40%, catheter 25%). Every skill is then topped up to **at least 2 carers per shift**. This alone does not guarantee feasibility: a visit needs its whole skill *combination* in one carer (e.g. medication + manual handling + catheter care). That is enforced by `validate_scenario()`, and the generator refuses to write a scenario that fails it. 20% of carers start at the office.
3. **Clients.** Needs are drawn at fixed rates (medication 60%, manual handling 20%, dementia 25%, catheter 10%). 15% of medication clients are time-critical, for example Parkinson's medication.
4. **Visits.** Each client gets a package of calls. Each call has a preferred time within its slot and an arrival window of ±30 minutes (±15 for time-critical medication). Durations are 30–60 minutes in the morning and 30 minutes at lunch and tea. There are no 15-minute calls: NICE quality standard QS123 (statement 4) expects visits of at least 30 minutes unless shorter ones are agreed in advance. Each call requires only the skills that apply to it: catheter and manual handling at morning and bed calls, medication and dementia care at every call.
5. **Preferences.** For 85% of clients, one early and one late carer are each chosen with probability 0.8, **only among carers qualified for all of the client's needs**. "Recent carers" are the preferred carers plus 1–3 other qualified carers.

`validate_scenario()` checks the result: references exist, there are no duplicates, and every visit has at least one carer with the skills and a shift that covers its window. A test confirms the committed JSON matches the generator output exactly.

## Known biases and limitations

- **Everyone drives.** Many real Birmingham carers walk or use buses, which changes travel times and feasible patches.
- **Continuous shifts only.** There are no split shifts, breaks or paid travel rules. Real rotas often use split shifts (morning and evening, unpaid gap), which matter for Tier 3 attrition features.
- **No double-up calls.** Two-carer hoist transfers are common in practice and need synchronised arrivals, which this prototype doesn't model.
- **Uniform demand rates.** Needs, packages and preferences are independent random draws. Real clients cluster (complex needs go with 4-call packages and narrower carer pools).
- **Preferences are always feasible** in skill terms, and recent carers are a random sample, not derived from a real history.
- **Straight-line patch geography.** Gaussian clusters ignore roads, rivers, canals and the motorway network. OSRM (Step 2.2) adds road travel times, but the client distribution itself is not realistic.
- **One day, one scenario.** Results from it are illustrative, not statistically general. Steps 2.4 and 2.5 should also report results on other seeds.
