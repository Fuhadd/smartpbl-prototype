# Data card: synthetic carer workforce (`train.csv`, `test.csv`, `oracle.csv`)

## Summary

1,000 synthetic carers at a snapshot date. Each carer has 12 features summarising the previous 13 weeks, plus a label saying whether they left in the following 12 months. Generated by `scripts/generate_workforce.py` (seed 42) from per-carer-day working conditions produced by the Tier 2 solver (`tier2_carer_days.json`, from `scripts/calibrate_workforce.py`). It is the input to every Tier 3 experiment.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Carers | 1,000 (`WF-0001` … `WF-1000`; no names, no locations) |
| Leavers within 12 months | 298 (29.8%) |
| Train / test | 800 / 200, stratified on the label (`train_test_split`, `random_state=42`); 60 leavers in test |
| Zero-hours contracts | 371 (37.1%); leaver rate 41.8% vs 22.7% for contracted carers |
| Oracle ROC-AUC (best achievable from these features) | **0.726** on all carers; **0.795** on the seed-42 test split |

**The test split is unusually easy.** Across 200 random stratified 20% splits, the oracle's test AUC averages 0.729 (95% range 0.645–0.795). The seed-42 split sits at the 97th percentile. The split rule was fixed before generation, so it was kept rather than re-drawn (re-drawing until the test set looks "typical" is still selecting a test set by its results). **Consequence for Step 3.2:** report test metrics next to the oracle on the same 200 rows, and treat cross-validation on the training set as the main estimate.

## How it was generated

1. **Working conditions come from the solver.** Each carer is given a "home round": one of the 84 working carer-days from the Tier 2 solves. For each of their shifts over 13 weeks (shifts per week × 13), the shift is that home round with probability 0.6, or else a random solved day on the same rota (covering other rounds). Driving, gaps, visits, split-shift share and familiarity are averages over those shifts. The solves are run once and committed, because time-limited search varies between runs (note 13). Re-solving would make the dataset non-reproducible.
2. **Employment features are stated assumptions,** not estimates from any real provider:
   - **Age band:** 18–24 12%, 25–39 33%, 40–54 33%, 55+ 22%.
   - **Tenure:** lognormal, σ = 0.9. Median 9 / 18 / 30 / 42 months by age band, capped by age.
   - **Zero-hours:** 55% / 40% / 35% / 30% by age band, about 37% overall. Anchor: about 38% of the English domiciliary workforce in 2023/24, as reported by the Home Care Association citing Skills for Care (secondary source; the original figure was not checked).
   - **Shifts per week:** contracted 4–5; zero-hours 2–5.
   - **Short-notice changes:** Poisson, mean 1.0 a month (contracted) or 2.5 (zero-hours).
3. **The label comes from a known model.** Log-odds of leaving = intercept + the terms below + U, where U ~ N(0, 1) is an unobserved factor (caring duties, a better offer, health) that no feature contains. The intercept (−1.828) is solved by bisection so the expected leaver rate is 30%. Anchor: sector-wide turnover is about 25% (Skills for Care, independent sector 2024/25, as reported secondhand), and older editions put domiciliary care higher (38.8% in 2018/19). 30% sits between the two; it is an assumption, not PBL's rate.

| Ground-truth term | Effect on log-odds | SD of contribution across carers |
|---|---|---|
| New starter (tenure < 6 months) | +1.2 | 0.31 |
| First year (6–11 months) | +0.6 | 0.23 |
| Long tenure | −0.3 per log-year beyond 1 year | 0.24 |
| Zero-hours contract | +0.5 | 0.24 |
| Driving | +0.025 per minute/shift above 40 | 0.23 |
| Unpaid gaps | +0.008 per minute/shift above 90 | 0.23 |
| Split-shift share | +0.8 × share | 0.25 |
| Familiar clients | −1.2 × (share − 0.55) | 0.20 |
| Short-notice changes | +0.3 per change/month above 2 | 0.25 |
| … extra if zero-hours (interaction) | +0.3 per change above 2 | 0.23 |
| **No effect:** `late_shift`, `skills_count`, `visits_per_shift`, `shifts_per_week`, `weekly_paid_hours`, `age_band` | 0 | 0 |
| Latent U (unobserved) | N(0, 1) | **1.00** |

The shapes are deliberate tests of the Tier 3 pipeline:
- **Steps and hinges** (tenure, short-notice changes) and an **interaction** (zero-hours × changes): a linear model can't represent them exactly; gradient boosting can.
- **Null features (five, plus age):** a sound explanation method should give them near-zero importance. `weekly_paid_hours` was missing from the null list until Step 3.3 (the data was unaffected).
- **Correlated features** (gaps and split shifts both come from idle time): importance may be shared or swapped between them.
- **A strong unobserved factor:** U has about 4× the spread of any single observed term, and about the same spread as all observed terms combined (SD of observed log-odds 0.97).
- **Irreducible noise has two sources.** With U removed, the oracle AUC is still only 0.754, because a carer with a 40% chance of leaving may or may not leave: the outcome is a coin flip at moderate probabilities. U lowers the ceiling further, to 0.726 (with U at SD 2 it would be 0.683). Real attrition is like this. Much of why people leave is never in the HR system.

The direction of each effect follows the qualitative picture in UK care workforce reporting (early leavers, insecure contracts, unpaid travel and gaps, short-notice rota changes). **The sizes are invented.**

`age_band` has no direct effect, yet leaver rates differ by age band: 46.8% (18–24), 31.3% (25–39), 26.4% (40–54), 23.3% (55+). The gap arrives through tenure and contract type, which are correlated with age. This is the proxy-discrimination case audited in Step 3.3.

## Known biases and limitations

- **Circularity.** Any model trained on this data learns the generator. Good metrics show that the pipeline recovers a known mechanism. They say nothing about why real carers leave or how well a model would predict that. See note 16.
- **Assumed, independent employment features.** In reality, tenure, contract, hours and rota changes are entangled (zero-hours carers are offered fewer shifts and get more changes). Only the contract → shifts and contract → changes links are modelled.
- **Working conditions come from 5 synthetic days and 17 rota slots.** Variety between carers is limited to 84 home rounds plus mixing. Weekends, seasons and bank holidays are not modelled.
- **No censoring.** Every carer is observed for the full 12 months after the snapshot. Real data has carers hired recently or observed for less than a year, which calls for survival analysis or careful label windows.
- **No time dimension.** A single snapshot. Real data would need features from before each snapshot only, and a time-based split (train on earlier years, test on later).
- **A label without causes.** Leaving is one event. Real data would distinguish voluntary leavers, dismissals and retirement, which should not be pooled for a retention model.
- **Synthetic identities only.** `WF-` references are unrelated to the `CR-` carers in Tier 2.
